NEW LAMPS FOR OLD

a tale from the Thousand and One Nights

Once there was a careless boy, the son of a poor tailor, who found an old copper lamp in an enchanted cave. A sorcerer from the Maghreb had sent him down to fetch it, then sealed him inside when the boy would not hand it over. In the dark the boy rubbed the ring the sorcerer had lent him, and a servant of the ring carried him home.

His mother rubbed the lamp to polish it for the market, and a far greater spirit came out. With the genie's help the boy grew rich, built a palace in a single night, and married the Sultan's daughter, the princess Badroulbadour.

But the sorcerer came back. He walked under the palace windows with a basket of shining new lamps, crying: "New lamps for old! Who will trade an old lamp for a new one?" The princess laughed at the fool, and she traded the dusty old lamp from the shelf. That night the palace flew to Africa.

All the boy had left was the ring. Storytellers always remember the lamp. They forget that the ring saved him twice.
